Day 1 of 3

Today you build the brain.

An AI employee is useless until it knows two things: who it is writing for, and how you sound. That is the brain. You build it in three steps, install the twelve specialists that use it, and publish one post before the session ends.

Before you start

  • Claude paid account, signed in. The free plan cannot install skills
  • A microphone you can talk into, or Wispr Flow
  • LinkedIn open in a tab
Say your answers out loud today

Two of the three steps ask you to talk rather than type. People write in a stiffer voice than they speak in, and that stiffness ends up in every post your employee writes for the next month.

First, 2 minutes

Make the folder everything lives in.

A Claude Project is a folder with a memory. Everything you build across the three days goes in this one project.

1
Claude sidebar → Projects → New project
Name it exactly this, so it is easy to find on Day 3:
Project name
AI Marketing Employee
No description needed.
2
Do all three brain steps inside it
Every chat you start today should be inside this project. One project for the whole challenge, not one per exercise.
The main work of Day 1

Three steps. Three documents. One brain.

Each one is its own page with the questions and a copy button. Do them in order, because Step 3 needs what Steps 1 and 2 produce.

1
Your ICP≈ 15 minutes

Eight plain questions about the person you sell to. Answer them, press copy, paste into Claude. You get back a full customer profile.

You finish with: ICP.md saved on your computer.

Open Step 1 · Your ICP
2
Your Voice DNA≈ 25 minutes

Five questions you answer out loud, four or five minutes each. No editing, no tidying up. Press copy, paste into Claude.

You finish with: VOICE-DNA.md saved on your computer.

Open Step 2 · Your Voice DNA
3
Your Business Brain≈ 5 minutes

Paste the two documents in, press copy, paste into Claude. It welds them into one file and adds the rules about what your employee must never do.

You finish with: BUSINESS-BRAIN.md sitting in your Project knowledge. That is the brain installed.

Open Step 3 · Your Business Brain
One entry in Project knowledge, not three

The Business Brain already contains your ICP and your voice. Adding all three files means Claude reads the same thing three times in three slightly different versions and gets less decisive, not more. Keep the other two on your computer as backups.

About 12 minutes

Install your twelve specialists.

A skill is a folder of instructions Claude loads when the job calls for it. Instead of one assistant that is average at everything, you get twelve that are each very good at one thing. They all read the brain you just built.

Everything below is in this folder. Sign into Google first or it will not open.

Open the skills folder

How to install one

Forty seconds each. Do the first one slowly, then repeat.

1
Download the .skill file from Drive
Three dots → Download. Do not unzip it.
2
In Claude: Customize → Skills
Left sidebar, about the sixth item down.
3
Click + → Create skill → Upload skill
Uploading lives under "Create skill", which is not obvious. Drag the file in. Two seconds.
4
Repeat for all twelve

How you actually use one

There is no button. You mention the skill in a normal sentence and Claude loads it. That is the whole mechanism, and it is the part people miss.

Name it directly

Most reliable
Use my linkedin-post-writer skill and write
a post about [topic].

Or describe the job

Also works
Write me a LinkedIn carousel about [topic],
using whichever of my skills fits best.
If nothing seems to happen

Claude did not pick it up. Say it more plainly: Use the skill called linkedin-post-writer. Names are exactly as written in the table, hyphens included.

About 15 minutes

Publish something today.

Not tomorrow once it is perfect. The point is not the post, it is proving to yourself that the brain you just built produces something you will put your name on.

Paste into a chat inside your project
Use my linkedin-post-writer skill.

Write a post about one person who taught me something
I still carry with me today.

Here is the raw story, unpolished:
[two or three sentences about the person and what they taught you]

Use my Business Brain in project knowledge for my voice and
my customer. 1300 to 1500 characters. End with a question
that invites a reply, not a pitch.

Read it out loud. If a sentence is one you would never say, tell Claude which one and why. Two rounds of that is usually enough.

Publish it

  • You could read it aloud without wincing
  • A detail only you would know
  • Written to one person

Send it back

  • Could be about any business
  • Short punchy sentences stacked for drama
  • A tidy lesson at the end nobody asked for

Never posted on LinkedIn before?

Better starting point than it feels like: no bad habits to unlearn, no audience expecting a particular version of you. Same exercise, one change. Make the post about why you are starting now. That is honest, genuinely interesting, and it removes the pressure to sound like an authority on day one, which is what actually stops people pressing publish.

Then post it and drop the link in chat

This is the show-and-tell that opens Day 2.

If you are stuck on anything
